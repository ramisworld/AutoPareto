## 2. What the records showed, and how the paper handles it

**The 1,024-in / 128-out decode benchmark was run only on the starting model and Opus's three recipes.** AR-ref, GPT A22/A24/A25, TPE and MOTPE have decode and memory records only on the original 256-token benchmark. No archived record exists for them on the newer benchmark. I checked the raw re-measurement repeats, the ablation session, the fresh-A40 G1/G2 runs and the decode diagnostics.

You asked for 1,024/128 throughout, so the paper handles it this way:

- Every decode and memory value in a Part 1 figure uses that benchmark.
- The six recipes without a record have empty cells, explained by one note. No value is estimated.
- The 256-token numbers appear only in two places:
  - the appendix scatterplot, on its own panel and axis;
  - one Part 1 sentence that keeps GPT A22's 1.99× decode result, explicitly labelled as the original benchmark. That sentence supports the abstract, which I left unchanged.

**Why Opus pursued inference code and GPT-5.6 did not.** These are recorded decisions under the same hook, quoted verbatim in Figure 2 and §1.4:

- Both agents first shrank the model, reaching about 1.9× decode on their own benchmarks (GPT A22; Opus A4).
- Opus then recorded a quality limit: "the quality prediction was falsified" (A7).
- It diagnosed launch-bound decoding and wrote that "CUDA-graph replay is the only lever that can raise speed without a quality cost" (A10).
- GPT-5.6's hypotheses tied decode speed to model size, for example "materially improving cached decode speed" by removing a layer (A4). It used the hook in 0 of 75 original and 0 of 22 later attempts.

The paper reports this as an observed strategy difference, not a property of either model: each search ran once, under different harness conditions.

## 6. Remaining scientific issues

1. **Decode comparisons across benchmarks.** GPT and AR-ref recipes cannot be compared with Opus on decode speed or memory. Re-running the 1,024/128 benchmark on their existing checkpoints would fill Figure 1's empty cells, but it is a new GPU measurement and needs your approval.
2. **Seed groups.** Figure 1 mixes seed groups by design: seeds 3 to 5 for the original recipes and 6 to 8 for Opus. Marker shapes and the caption say so. AR-ref and GPT A22 replicate within 0.001 BPB across the two groups.
3. **Ratios in Figure 2.** GPT A22's "decode 1.9×" in the lineage is a ratio to its own starting model on the 256-token search benchmark. The caption states this.
4. **Opus path in Figure 4a.** It shows cumulative campaign records, not isolated effects. A18, A19 and A22 were flagged speed-uncertain and are hatched.
5. **Unchanged limitations:**
   - one run per agent;
   - one GPU type and small models;
   - correctness checks miss the timed window and the ring cache;
   - downstream differences are within seed noise;
   - random search, NSGA-II and CMA-ES were never retrained, so they appear only in the approaches table with their discovery scores.
6. **Your screenshots.** The two screenshots mentioned in the brief did not reach me. Figure 1 and the scatterplot follow your written description. The lineage follows the tree style of the GPT-only lineage figure from revision 8.
