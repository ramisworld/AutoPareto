## 2. Why 5A needed two documented changes
The E1 extension pre-registered on 2026-11-02 (`E1_OPUS_EXTENSION_PREREG.md`) is **not executable**. Its frozen adapter
(`scripts/pair_a_to_phase1_source.py`, unchanged) refuses all three Opus sources with "expected exactly one anchor:
'total_tokens = step * TOTAL_BATCH_SIZE'". It stays unrun, as that plan's rule requires.

Phase 5A instead uses two scripted, gated steps:
1. **Driver mapping** (`scripts/opus_contract_t_to_phase1_source.py`).
   - It restores the panel-baseline text of the Contract-T loop driver: 65 changed lines for attempts 23 and 24, 73 for
     attempt 27.
   - The full diffs are in `evaluation/phase5a_sources/*.mapping.diff`.
   - Gates, all passing: definitions are byte-identical except the `prepare` import; only the expected statement-level
     changes are present; the protected transform accepts seeds 6–8; the driver edit is identical across recipes.
2. **Deployment step** (`scripts/phase5a_export_deployed_checkpoints.py`). The recipe's own post-training storage
   statements are applied before scoring:
   - bf16 matrices for all three recipes;
   - int8 gather tables for attempt 27.

   Without this step, the protected loader would truncate attempt 27's bf16 tables to int8.

All three recipes already use TOTAL_BATCH_SIZE 2^17 and DEVICE_BATCH_SIZE 64, like AR-ref and AP-Q. Tokens (201,326,592),
updates (1,536) and the loader's token stream are therefore identical to E1's 201.3M cell.

**LR semantics.** Contract T used time progress; the token cell uses token fraction, as AR-ref and AP-Q did in E1.

**Run time and cost.** The chain ran from 05:53:09 to 11:35:39 UTC, **5.71 A40-h**, about $2.5 at $0.44/h:
- 5A training: 1.92 h;
- downstream suite: 0.69 h;

### 3.1 5A: matched tokens (201,326,592 tokens, 1,536 updates, seeds 6–8, paired with E1)

**Drift control.** AR-ref seed 6 gave held-out 1.0938 against E1's 1.0947 (Δ −0.0010) and validation Δ −0.0004, with identical tokens and updates. That is inside the 0.005 rule, so the session is **not confounded**. The checkpoint is not bit-identical, because the kernels are nondeterministic. Same-seed noise is therefore about 0.001 BPB.

| Recipe | Params | Train tok/s | Held-out BPB, mean ± sd [95% CI] | Δ vs AR-ref [95% CI] | Δ vs AP-Q [95% CI] |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| AR-ref | 50.3M | 289k | 1.0995 ± 0.0042 | — | — |
| AP-Q | 29.4M | 523k | 1.1410 ± 0.0036 | +0.0415 [+0.029, +0.054] | — |
| Opus 23 | 26.2M | 658k | 1.1582 ± 0.0022 | **+0.0587 [+0.051, +0.066]** | +0.017 [+0.004, +0.030] |
| Opus 24 | 22.0M | 658k | 1.1603 ± 0.0011 | **+0.0608 [+0.052, +0.070]** | +0.019 [+0.008, +0.031] |
| Opus 27 | 26.2M | 657k | 1.1580 ± 0.0020 | **+0.0586 [+0.053, +0.064]** | +0.017 [+0.006, +0.028] |

**Primary outcome.** All three Opus recipes are **confirmed worse than AR-ref** at matched tokens.
- Every CI excludes 0.
- Holm-adjusted p = 0.002 for each.
- All 9 seed pairs are worse, by +0.056 to +0.065.

Against AP-Q they are also confirmed worse, by about 0.017–0.019. Validation BPB agrees in sign and size.

**Downstream suite** (descriptive; seeds 6–8):
- Opus is **lower on BLiMP** than AR-ref, by 0.034–0.038, with every CI excluding 0.
- Opus is indistinguishable from AR-ref on LAMBADA, PIQA and ARC-E.
- Opus is indistinguishable from AP-Q on every task.

**Training throughput and exchange rate** (exploratory, pre-registered):
- ρ = 2.27–2.28: Opus trains that many times AR-ref's tokens per second.
- At 201.3M tokens, Opus's held-out BPB is worse than AR-ref's at 88.6M on every seed. The exchange rate k therefore lies beyond AR-ref's measured range: **k > 2.27**, or 2.38–2.46 by linear extrapolation in ln tokens.
- Since ρ ≲ k, Opus is **not predicted to beat AR-ref under the fixed 300 s contract** in this harness.

**Post hoc, descriptive.** Opus's 300 s exposure at its measured throughput (about 197M tokens) is within 2% of the 201.3M cell. Opus at 201.3M minus AR-ref at 300 s (E1, about 88M tokens), paired by seed:

| Recipe | Mean Δ [95% CI] |
|---|---|
| Opus 23 | +0.0013 [−0.004, +0.007] |
| Opus 24 | +0.0034 [−0.005, +0.012] |
| Opus 27 | +0.0011 [−0.005, +0.007] |

That is a statistical tie: Opus's 2.3× throughput buys back almost exactly its per-token deficit. The same pattern appeared for AP-Q in E1.

**Interpretation.** At equal tokens and updates, the three-layer Opus architectures are less sample-efficient than both AR-ref and AP292. Their Contract-T advantage comes from throughput, against the Pair-A baseline it was scored against. It is not an advantage per token, and it does not establish a fixed-time win over AR-ref.

### 3.2 5B: what produces the Opus inference speed-up (existing checkpoints, no retraining)

**Correctness.**
- Correctness passed in all 39 runs, on either the bf16 fast path or FP32 adjudication.
- For each checkpoint, the greedy sample is byte-identical across all six variants.
- The same-session baseline gave 145, 149 and 153 tok/s at the start, middle and end of the session.

**Decode (multiplicative factors, mean of 2 repeats).** These factors agree across attempts 23, 24 and 27 to within about 0.1× in every case except the joint graph+fusion factor, which ranges from 15.6× to 16.7×.

| Factor | Decode | End-to-end |
|---|---|---|
| Total, V0 / same-session baseline | **30.7–30.8×** | **5.2–5.4×** |
| CUDA graph, V0/V1 | 11.2–11.7× | 1.9–2.0× |
| Inductor fusion, V0/V2 | 2.7–2.8× | 1.0–1.1× |
| Graph and fusion jointly, V0/V4 | 15.6–16.7× | 2.6–2.8× |
| Custom split-key attention, V0/V3 | 1.29× | 1.0× |
| Ring K/V buffer, V0/V5 | 1.05× (K/V 3.6 vs 7.5 MiB) | 1.0× |
| Architecture plus Opus's eager decode code, V4/baseline | 1.85–1.97× | 1.85–1.97× |

**Prefill.**
- V0: 131–138 ms.
- No graph (V1): 3.7–4.3 ms.
- Baseline: 7.3 ms.

The per-prompt warm-up and capture account for about 97% of Opus's prefill time. Without them, Opus prefills about 2× faster than the baseline.

**Kernel census** (attempt 23; per decode token):

| Model or variant | GPU kernels | Host launch calls |
|---|---|---|
| Baseline | 625 | 618 |
| Opus, eager and unfused | 290 | 284 |
| Opus, fused, no graph | 78 | 75 |
| Opus, as submitted | 78 | 1 graph launch + 1 call |

Batch-1 decode is launch-bound:
- the architecture halves the kernel count;
- fusion cuts it about 3.7× further;
- the CUDA graph removes the per-kernel host cost.

**Stability.**
- 5 of 36 variant runs failed the frozen stability rule. All 5 are eager (V1 or V4) runs, which are CPU-launch-bound on this host, the one that failed the CPU screen.
- All graph variants and all 3 baseline runs were stable.
- Nothing was re-run.

**Predictions.** P1–P6, recorded before measurement, all held.

**Interpretation.**
- The 31× decode-only speed-up factors as about 2× × about 16×:
  - about 2× from the three-layer architecture together with Opus's eager decode code;
  - about 16× from inference engineering: a CUDA graph captured per prompt (about 11×) and Inductor-fused decode regions (about 2.7×), which combine sub-multiplicatively.

  Neither engineering mechanism depends on the architecture. Applying them to the baseline was not tested here.
- The decode-only metric rewards this design. The graph is captured inside the untimed prefill, so the end-to-end gain is only about 5×.
- Fusion and the custom attention kernel change end-to-end throughput by no more than about 7%.
